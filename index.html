ABOUT RESERVOIRS

What is a Water Reservoir?

Water Reservoir

A water reservoir is a natural or artificial storage area used to collect and store water. Dams and lakes are common examples of reservoirs.

Reservoirs help communities manage water resources throughout the year. Stored water can be released when water demand is high or rainfall is low.

Reservoirs play an important role in water conservation, agriculture, electricity production and drinking water supply.

WATER STORAGE

Store Water For The Future

Rainwater Collection

Rainwater flows into rivers and reservoirs where it can be collected and stored.

Water Storage

Large reservoirs store huge amounts of water for use throughout the year.

Summer Supply

Stored water can be released during summer when rainfall and river flow may decrease.

AGRICULTURE

Water For Farming

Reservoirs Support Agriculture

Reservoir water is supplied to agricultural fields through canals, pipelines and irrigation systems.

Farmers use this water for growing crops, especially during periods when rainfall is insufficient.

HYDROELECTRIC POWER

Clean Energy From Water

How Hydropower Works

Water stored at a higher level in a reservoir has potential energy. When water flows through a dam, it moves turbines.

The turbines rotate generators which produce electricity. This electricity can then be supplied to homes, schools and industries.

DRINKING WATER

Safe Water For Communities

Reservoirs Provide Drinking Water

Reservoirs store water that can be treated and supplied to cities, towns and villages. Water treatment plants remove impurities before the water reaches households.

Reliable reservoir storage helps maintain water supply during dry periods.
